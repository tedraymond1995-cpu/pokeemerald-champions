# Pokémon Emerald Champions: notes for Claude

A hardcore Emerald ROM hack in the style of Pokémon Run & Bun, built on RHH's pokeemerald-expansion (master after 1.17.2). The repo owner leads design and playtests in mGBA. Claude does most of the implementation. The owner is new to ROM hacking, so explain changes in plain words and get approval before big design changes.

## Design rules (approved)

- Difficulty matches Run & Bun. The player gets a bit more power (setup moves, customization), and fights are tuned around it.
- Keep the Emerald world and story feel. Routing forces mandatory trainers; optional trainers are tricky but easier. Use back-to-back battles and gauntlets.
- Gym order is Run & Bun's: Brawly, Roxanne, Wattson, Norman, Flannery, Winona, Tate & Liza, Juan. Splits follow Run & Bun's too (Brawly, Roxanne, Wattson, Norman, Fallarbor, Flannery, Winona, Mt. Pyre, Hideouts, Tate & Liza, Space Center/Seafloor, Juan, Victory Road), with two splits before Brawly: Aqua Grunt (ends at the first boss, the Aqua grunt in Petalburg Woods) and Museum (ends at the Slateport museum).
- Story before Brawly: the first boss is the Aqua grunt in Petalburg Woods (`TRAINER_GRUNT_PETALBURG_WOODS`, 3 Pokémon). The scene path there is one tile wide: Team Aqua steals the Devon Goods there, and the Devon researcher then blocks the way to Rustboro until Capt. Stern has them back. Mr. Briney only sails after that scene. His first trip passes Dewford and goes straight to Slateport (Route 109), and he won't sail from there until the museum is done. The museum is a back-to-back boss (two Aqua grunts, no healing between); the player gets the parts back, returns them to Stern, and Stern hands over the Letter for Steven. Then Briney sails to Dewford, but won't sail back to Petalburg until Brawly's badge. Brawly's gym is closed (a Black Belt stands in front of the door) until the Letter is delivered to Steven. Steven skips the PokéNav registration if the player has no PokéNav (he is registered silently).
- Still vanilla and in need of rework for this order: the Rustboro theft, Rusturf Tunnel and Mr. Stone's errands (the Devon Goods and the Letter are already done by then), and Birch's PokéNav registration on Route 110 (skipped when the player has no PokéNav). The Route 110 rival battle still triggers on the first pass. NPCs who talk about planting berries (the Pretty Petal flower shop, which also still gives the Wailmer Pail, and the Chesto Berry woman on Route 104) still say planting works.
- Level caps are hard (no EXP at the cap), listed in play order in `src/caps.c`. Decided: 12 at the first boss (the woods Aqua grunt), 16 at the Slateport museum, 20 at gym 1, 24 at gym 2, 98 for the Elite Four. The other values are a draft.
- 12 Rare Candies placed in the whole game, scarce or earned. One early wild Pokémon also holds a Rare Candy, to appeal to players who are not doing a Nuzlocke: the owner picked Zigzagoon, as a nod to Pickup. A wild Zigzagoon (the Hoenn form only, not the Galarian form or Linoone) always holds one (`itemCommon` and `itemRare` in `src/data/pokemon/species_info/gen_3_families.h`; tests in `test/wild_held_items.c`). The owner named the Pokémon but not the odds: 'always' matches Luvdisc's Heart Scale and is one line to change. They can go over the cap (`B_RARE_CANDY_CAP FALSE`): 12 = 6 Pokémon × 2 levels, so saving all of them gives a level 100 team for the E4.
- No grinding: the Endless Candy (`ITEM_ENDLESS_CANDY`, key item, Birch gives it with the starter) raises a level by one per use, stops at the cap, and is never used up.
- Pokémon Center services attendant, as in Run & Bun: a receptionist at (12,2) in every Pokémon Center 1F (not the Battle Frontier's; Verdanturf's Expert moved to (13,4)) runs `PokemonCenter_EventScript_Services` (`data/scripts/pokemon_center_services.inc`; specials in `src/pokemon_center_services.c`; prices and menu rows in `include/constants/pokemon_center_services.h`; tests in `test/pokemon_center_services.c`). Relearn a level-up move: 1 Heart Scale. Forget a move: free. Max out one IV to 31: 1 Heart Scale each, refused if it's already 31. Change nature: 3 Heart Scales, done like a Mint (`MON_DATA_HIDDEN_NATURE`), so gender, ability and shininess stay, and the summary screen names the nature the stats use. Change nickname: free, Name Rater rules. Give a party Pokémon a status: free; Burn, Freeze, Paralysis, Poison or Sleep, replacing any status it had; refused for fainted Pokémon and for types and abilities that prevent it; sleep gets the longest counter (asleep for 6 turns, as in Run & Bun). Pokémon can come from the party or the PC, except for statuses. There will be more Heart Scales than in Run & Bun. Placed ones are finite rewards, but the owner is fine with players who are not doing a Nuzlocke farming them: a wild Luvdisc always holds one (it is in Littleroot's pond).
- Gimmicks: Megas only. Never give the player or any trainer Z-Moves, Dynamax or Tera.
- HMs work from the bag, as in Run & Bun (`OW_HM_FIELD_MOVES_FROM_BAG` in `include/config/overworld.h`): the HM plus its badge are enough and no Pokémon needs to know the move. A party Pokémon that knows the move still uses it; otherwise the first one that could learn it (else the first one) shows up as the user (`GetHMFieldMoveUser` in `src/field_move.c`); FLY and FLASH show up for every Pokémon in the party menu once the player has the HM. HM moves can be forgotten or replaced (`P_CAN_FORGET_HIDDEN_MOVE TRUE`, `B_CATCH_SWAP_CHECK_HMS FALSE`). Tests in `test/hm_from_bag.c`.
- No EVs anywhere (`EV_CAP_NO_GAIN` plus `B_EV_ITEMS_CAP TRUE`). Trainer parties use no EVs.
- No weather or terrain for the player: no setter abilities on catchable Pokémon, and setter moves are removed from what the player's Pokémon can learn. Enemies may use them, and some fights start with a permanent field effect (`Starting Status` in `trainers.party`).
- Affection effects are off (`B_AFFECTION_MECHANICS FALSE`): random survival and crits break planning.
- Summary screen (`include/config/summary_screen.h`, `contest.h`): IVs shown as numbers (press L on the stats page), no contest moves page, and no free move relearner there, since relearning costs a Heart Scale.
- Trainer AI follows Run & Bun-style rules that players can read and plan around. Document every rule.
- A deathless run must stay possible but very hard: every boss keeps at least one deathless line.
- Keep Run & Bun's ratio of doubles to singles.
- New content goes in two zones: gyms 1–3 (especially between gyms 1 and 2) and a new section between gym 8 and Victory Road. Later-generation characters appear as bosses. Use Emerald's own tilesets so new areas look native.
- Full player documentation ("open book"): every trainer, encounter and item.
- Battle style is locked to Set, and the bag can't be used in trainer battles (Run & Bun rules; `IsAllowedToUseBag` in `src/battle_util.c`).
- No wild battles while the party is empty (`ShouldDisableRandomEncounters` in `src/field_control_avatar.c`), as a safety net before the starter.
- Early encounters: Littleroot is water only (a small pond). The grass is in the Little Grotto south of town (`LittlerootTown_Grotto`, its own area), which twins block until the player has Poké Balls (`FLAG_ADVENTURE_STARTED`). Slateport's Battle Tent lobby is its own area too (`MAPSEC_SLATEPORT_BATTLE_TENT`): a Pokéfan there gives Eevee (level 10, 3 perfect IVs) with a random Fire, Water or Thunder Stone. Professor Birch gives the Infinite Repel (`ITEM_INFINITE_REPEL`, key item that toggles `FLAG_INFINITE_REPEL_ON`; it works like a Repel that never wears off) and the Endless Candy with the starter. The Super Rod comes from a fisherman on the rock at Route 103's west shore (`Route103_EventScript_SuperRodFisherman`, `FLAG_RECEIVED_SUPER_ROD`). The first Poké Balls (10) come from the rival after the Route 103 battle. The Oldale footprints man gives the Johto starter of the third type at level 5 (Totodile, Chikorita or Cyndaquil). The starter and the Oldale gift get 3 random perfect IVs (the `SetGiftMonPerfectIVs` special does it for gifts, right after `givemon`). Early water areas have placeholder Super Rod tables; later ones still have vanilla late-game levels.
- Route 103 uses Run & Bun's layout (credited in `README.md`): a rock with stairs on each shore and Acro Bike rails across the water between them, so the Acro Bike gives a shortcut from Oldale to the east side and Route 110. The east-side trainers stand where Run & Bun puts them, Fisherman Andrew guards the east rock, and the two swimmers only battle when talked to.
- Mandatory trainers follow the route script; `/home/claude/scripts/mandatory_report.py` checks that a trainer can't be walked around. Route 102: Calvin (south path) and Allen (northwest gap). Route 104 south uses Run & Bun's trees that close the meadow, so the way to the woods runs Billy (foot of the beach stairs), Darian (beach), Cindy (path to the woods). Route 109's beach uses Run & Bun's rows of beach chairs: Chandler and Lola (the 2 solos), Huey and Edmond side by side (the double), Ricky and Hailey (sight lines cross at (22,7), so a careless step gives a double battle). Route 106: Elliot on the beach and Ned beside Run & Bun's boulders guard the way to Granite Cave. Granite Cave 1F: the new Hiker Silas (`TRAINER_SILAS`, ID 855) guards the passage to the ladder. Dewford Gym uses Run & Bun's maze, and its trainers see in every direction (`TRAINER_TYPE_SEE_ALL_DIRECTIONS`, as in Run & Bun's gyms): Brenden and Lilith, then Cristian and Takao, are the two doubles (their sight lines meet), Laura and Jocelyn the solos. Roxanne split: James guards the west passage of the woods past the old block; on Route 104 north Winston, Gina & Mia (the true double, on the bridge) and Haley are mandatory. The Trainers tab's Role column says Mandatory or Optional.
- Berries are finite (`OW_BERRY_FINITE` and `OW_BERRY_TREE_YIELD_MIN`/`MAX` in `include/config/overworld.h`): every tree starts ripe and stays ripe until picked (`OW_BERRY_IMMORTAL`), holds a fixed 66-99 berries (worked out from the tree and the save's trainer ID by `GetFiniteBerryTreeYield` in `src/berry.c`, so it can't be rerolled and needs no save space), never regrows, and soil can't be planted (`BerryTree_EventScript_CheckSoil` in `data/scripts/berry_tree.inc`). The nine daily berry gifts are one-time: their `FLAG_DAILY_*BERRY*` flags keep their names but moved out of the daily block to 0x2A-0x32 in `include/constants/flags.h`. Berry types per patch are still vanilla and may change.
- Optional trainers guard the berry patches; `/home/claude/scripts/guard_check.py` checks that a guard's sight covers every way to the patch without touching the main path. Rick guards Route 102's patch (in the pocket north of the ledges) from (20,2) facing down. On Route 104: Tiana (moved from Route 102) guards the south patch from (25,42) facing left; Lyle (moved from Petalburg Woods, so split 4 now) guards the north-west patch by the flower shop from (4,22) facing down, behind a new fence on row 21; Ivan guards the north-east patch from (33,8) facing up. Not guarded yet: Route 110's patch (reachable in the museum split) and Route 116's; Route 103's east patch sits behind Cut trees.
- More encounter areas from the route script, all with placeholder tables until the encounter session: Dewford Town has Run & Bun's two small fenced gardens with tall grass (the gym door moved to (10,17) and the gym sign to (13,16), as in Run & Bun); Route 109 has three Rock Smash rocks in the beach's west pocket; and the PETAL GROTTO (`Route104_PetalGrotto`, `MAPSEC_PETAL_GROTTO`) is a flower meadow reached by a path west of the Pretty Petal flower shop on Route 104 (a map connection; Route 104 rows 18-19 meet grotto rows 6-7).
- Honey trees: each one is a one-time wild encounter that takes its area's encounter, so on a route with a tree the player chooses between the tree and that route's grass or water. A wooden HONEY TREE sign stands next to each tree: Route 102 (the lone tree south of the path), Petalburg Woods (the tree above the sign near the south entrance), Route 110 (a lone tree by the Slateport sign, reachable before the museum) and Route 106 (the tall tree on the beach). The player shakes the tree (YES/NO); the Pokémon comes from a weighted table at the current level cap minus 2, so a tree shaken later gives a stronger Pokémon. All trees share one table (Combee 20, Burmy 20, Cherubi 15, Aipom 15, Wurmple 15, Heracross 8, Munchlax 7, in percent); an area can get its own copy later. No Honey item is needed.
- Starters are Bulbasaur, Charmander and Squirtle (`src/starter_choose.c`). The rival uses the Gen 5 starters and takes the one that beats the player's: Tepig against Bulbasaur, Oshawott against Charmander, Snivy against Squirtle. The Route 103 rival has a second Pokémon, the Gen 3 starter that beats the Oldale gift (Treecko, Torchic or Mudkip). The rival trainer IDs keep their vanilla names, which describe the player's pick: `_TREECKO` means the player chose Bulbasaur, `_TORCHIC` means Charmander, `_MUDKIP` means Squirtle.
- The Super Rod is the only rod in the game: the Dewford and Route 118 fishermen no longer give the Old and Good Rod (the items still exist but can't be obtained). Each area has one fishing table that uses all 12 fishing slots with the same odds as land encounters (20/20/10/10/10/10/5/5/4/4/1/1, set in the `fields` of `src/data/wild_encounters.json`; `GetFishingSlotFromRoll` in `src/wild_encounter.c`; tests in `test/fishing.c`). A species gets a bigger share by filling several slots. Tables that are not redesigned yet were converted so their Super Rod odds stayed the same. Fishing is effortless (`I_FISHING_EFFORTLESS` in `include/config/fishing.h`): every cast hooks a Pokémon after three dots, with no button timing.
- No starter line of any generation appears in a wild, fishing or honey tree table. Starters are gifts that depend on the player's first pick: by the Elite Four the player should have one starter from each of 8 generations (the Kanto pick and the Oldale Johto gift are in the game; the other gifts are not placed yet, and the game has 9 starter trios, so ask the owner which 8 he means when the gifts are planned). The rival keeps its Gen 5 and Gen 3 starters, and the player never gets the same ones: the player's Gen 5 and Gen 3 gifts must be a different type from the rival's (with Bulbasaur not Tepig or Treecko, with Charmander not Oshawott or Torchic, with Squirtle not Snivy or Mudkip).
- Wild encounter tables are designed in the sheet's Encounters tab one split at a time and locked with the owner area by area (its Status column says Draft or Locked); only locked tables go into `src/data/wild_encounters.json`. The design counts one encounter per area, whatever the method (grass, fishing, Surf or a honey tree). Split 1 gives each area a type theme so early teams get type variety (the THEME notes in the sheet), with one fixed level per table, Hoenn natives in the common slots, fan favorites as rares, 1% long shots, and some families shared between areas on purpose. Inspiration: Run & Bun, Emerald Kaizo, other hacks, popularity votes and the sheet's Nuzlocke favorites tab. The Little Grotto gives the player a Fairy type early on purpose: early trainers can then use Dragons and the player still has counter-play.
- Limited resources stay limited for a Nuzlocke player. Pickup finds nothing after a battle (`B_PICKUP_AFTER_BATTLE FALSE` in `include/config/battle.h`; its in-battle effect is unchanged). Thief and Covet no longer steal items, as in Run & Bun, and Covet is Fairy type (`src/data/moves_info.h`), so trainers can hold powerful items. A heist stays possible, like the owner's Tate & Liza heist in Run & Bun: an item taken from a trainer's Pokémon with Magician (also copied with Role Play or Trace, and even on the hit that knocks the holder out), Trick or Switcheroo is kept after the battle when the thief started the battle with empty hands or a berry (tests in `test/battle/heist.c`). A Pokémon that started with a non-berry item of its own gets that item back after the battle, which undoes a swap; non-berry items that were used up or knocked off also come back (`B_RESTORE_HELD_BATTLE_ITEMS`, `B_TRAINERS_KNOCK_OFF_ITEMS`). Asked and not answered yet: use Run & Bun's rule instead, where nothing comes back after a battle. Items held by wild Pokémon stay in the game as a reward for a catch (`SetWildMonHeldItem` in `src/pokemon.c`). Asked and not answered yet: Trick, Switcheroo, Magician and Pickpocket can still take a wild Pokémon's item without a catch, which matters now that a wild Zigzagoon holds a Rare Candy. Not checked against this rule yet: Honey Gather, Pay Day in wild battles, trainer rematches, the Game Corner, Battle Tent prizes and the Lilycove lottery.
- Evolutions (direction decided, drafted for the owner, not built yet): no friendship, trade or similar special evolutions. As in Run & Bun, they become level-ups or evolution items, and the evolution items are released at chosen marts and points of the game. The draft is in the sheet: the Evolutions tab has one row for each of the 123 evolutions that are not a plain level-up or a plain item use today, with a proposed level or item (Status Draft, To decide for the 12 regional forms, Keep for the ones that stay), and the Evolution items tab lists each item, what it would evolve and a first release schedule by mart. Nothing changes in the game until the owner approves the list: he called the draft a good start and wants to go through it as cases come up, so don't build it as one batch. Found while drafting: held evolution items (King's Rock, Metal Coat, Razor Claw and so on) can't be used from the bag today (`I_USE_EVO_HELD_ITEMS_FROM_BAG FALSE` in `include/config/item.h`), so a solo player can't do any trade-with-item evolution until that is switched on; the game's region is always Hoenn, so the regional-form evolutions (`IF_REGION`: Alolan Raichu, Hisuian Typhlosion and so on) and Ursaluna can never happen; Wyrdeer and Hydrapple can't happen because Stantler and Dipplin can't learn Psyshield Bash and Dragon Cheer in this game; Runerigus needs a script trigger that no map calls; Tandemaus only evolves from a level gained in battle (`EVO_LEVEL_BATTLE_ONLY`), so not from the Endless Candy; and the Sweets (Alcremie) can't be used from the bag. A few wild Pokémon hold an evolution item 5% of the time (Cleffa a Moon Stone, Dratini a Dragon Scale), which gets around a mart lock unless the owner removes it. The Encounters tab's notes still describe the old methods and get updated once the list is approved.

## Where things live

- Design source for trainers and encounters: the owner's Google Sheet "Emerald Champions Master Sheet" (Trainers and Encounters tabs, one row per Pokémon). Its ID column names the `trainers.party` entry each row belongs to; one row can name several IDs that share a team (May and Brendan rival variants). The sheet is the source of truth for designed trainers: sync it into `trainers.party` rather than hand-editing those entries. Its other tabs: Evolutions and Evolution items (the evolution draft, see the design rules), Level caps (a copy of `src/caps.c` that the Evolutions tab's 'Earliest split' column looks up, so update it whenever a cap changes) and Nuzlocke favorites (about 50 Pokémon that players in a Reddit thread the owner shared came to like through Nuzlockes, with why, and where each is or could go in the encounter tables).
- The owner's route script (draft): the "Route script" tab of the plan doc (ROM Hack Plan). It sets the order, encounters, trainer counts and caps for each split.
- Trainer teams: `src/data/trainers.party` (Showdown export format, documented at the top of the file)
- New trainers get IDs at the end of `include/constants/opponents.h` (bump `TRAINERS_COUNT_EMERALD`). Only 8 free trainer IDs remain before trainer flags run out (`MAX_TRAINERS_COUNT_EMERALD` 864), so bigger batches need unused vanilla trainers reused or the flag space expanded.
- Wild encounters: `src/data/wild_encounters.json`; honey trees: tables in `src/honey_trees.c`, table IDs in `include/constants/honey_trees.h`, shared script and how to add a tree in `data/scripts/honey_trees.inc`, flags `FLAG_HONEY_TREE_*`
- Level caps: `src/caps.c`; cap and EV settings: `include/config/caps.h`
- Battle settings: `include/config/battle.h`; AI flags: `include/constants/battle_ai.h`
- Items: `include/constants/items.h`, `src/data/items.h`; Rare and Endless Candy use: `ItemUseCB_RareCandy` in `src/party_menu.c`
- New game setup: `src/new_game.c`
- Maps and event scripts: `data/maps/<Map>/` (`map.json`, `scripts.inc`). The owner edits map layouts in Porymap. A new map also needs its `scripts.inc` included in `data/event_scripts.s`, its name appended to a group in `data/maps/map_groups.json` (append, so existing map numbers don't shift), its layout in `data/layouts/layouts.json`, and, for a new area name, an entry appended to `src/data/region_map/region_map_sections.json` (16 characters at most).
- Battle and AI tests: `test/battle/` (for example `test/battle/ai/`)
- Playtest save, for moving around freely: `Playtest_EventScript_Setup` in `data/scripts/playtest.inc` gives all badges and HMs, marks every trainer beaten, opens every FLY spot, gives both bikes, the Super Rod, the Infinite Repel (on) and a level 100 team, and counts the story as done up to Wattson in this hack's order. It runs from the debug menu (hold R and press START, Utilities > Playtest setup) on any game. `tools/make_playtest_save.py` writes the same state as a ready-made save (a new game named TED, continuing outside Petalburg's Pokémon Center) by running `test/playtest_save.c` in the test ROM: `make pokeemerald-test.elf`, then `python3 tools/make_playtest_save.py pokeemerald-test.elf <name>.sav`. The save only fits builds with the same save layout (`test/save.c`); build a new one after save-block changes.

## Build

On Ubuntu (the cloud workspace):

```
sudo apt-get update
sudo apt-get install -y gcc-arm-none-eabi binutils-arm-none-eabi libnewlib-arm-none-eabi libpng-dev
make -j$(nproc)
```

A clean build takes about 8 minutes on 2 cores and produces `pokeemerald.gba`. `make check` runs the test suite (about 5,900 tests, about 45 minutes on 2 cores). Seven upstream tests fail because of this hack's rules and are expected: six in `test/battle/exp.c` (hard level caps stop EXP) and the Embargo EV test in `test/battle/move_effect/embargo.c` (no EVs). Everything else should pass.

## Workflow

- Work on a branch, push it, and open a pull request. The owner has approved Claude merging its own pull requests once the change is in a test build, so merge as you go and say so.
- After a build, give the owner the ROM to playtest: attach it in chat, and copy it into the build folder on their PC when the desktop app has that folder connected. The playtest save there is `emerald-champions-playtest.sav`; copied and renamed to match a ROM (same name, `.sav`), mGBA loads it with that ROM.
- Never commit ROMs or saves (`*.gba` and `*.sav` are gitignored). Releases ship as `.bps` patches.
- Keep the RHH credit line in `README.md`.
- Mark every hack-specific change in engine code with a short `Pokémon Emerald Champions` comment, so upstream merges stay easy.
